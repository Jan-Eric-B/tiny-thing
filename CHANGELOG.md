## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Project glossary and architecture decision records.
- Walking skeleton: the Tray core connects to the Device daemon over TCP, runs the Handshake (protocol and software versions, mismatches reported) and forwards the title and artist of Now Playing; the Device daemon passes them to the Device app, which shows them in the Simulator window.
- The Device daemon lets a new connection from the Tray app or the Device app replace the previous one, so a connection left half-open (USB unplugged, computer asleep) no longer blocks reconnecting. A Handshake that gets no answer gives up after 5 seconds instead of hanging.
- Continuous integration builds, lints and tests the workspace on Windows and Linux.
- The Tray app runs on Windows as a notification area icon with a Quit item and no window. It shows Now Playing from the Windows media controls on the Device: the title and artist of the Media session Windows marks as current, following song changes and switches between apps as they happen. A Media session without a title counts as nothing playing. When Windows fails to report Now Playing, the Tray app tries again after a short delay. When forwarding stops (for example the Device is not reachable), the tray icon's tooltip says why.
- Playback controls on the Device: the dial press and button 2 play or pause Now Playing, buttons 1 and 3 go to the previous and next song, and on-screen controls do the same by touch. The Device shows play or pause at once and keeps showing it until the Tray app reports that the player has played or paused (a new song alone does not end it); if that does not happen within 3 seconds it shows the last reported state again. In the Simulator, Enter stands in for the dial press and the keys 1, 2 and 3 for the buttons. Now Playing now carries whether it is playing or paused (protocol version 2). A press while no Tray app is connected is dropped rather than carried out later, and so is a press made while the player is still busy with one press and another is already waiting.
- Volume on the Device: turning the dial changes the computer's master Volume in 2 % steps, from 0 to 100 %, on the Windows default output device. The Device shows the Volume level while the dial turns or when the Volume changes on the computer, and hides it again 1.5 seconds after the last change. While the computer is muted, the Device shows a muted speaker; turning the dial unmutes it and changes the level, like the Windows volume keys. A slow player does not hold up Volume steps. When Windows fails to report the Volume, the Tray app keeps showing the last one and tries again after a short delay; Now Playing goes on either way. In the Simulator, the right and left arrow keys turn the dial, and so does the mouse wheel, one step per notch. The Tray app sends the Volume and the Device sends Volume steps (protocol version 3).
- Artwork on the Device: Now Playing shows the player's Artwork beside white text, on a Backdrop made from the Artwork, with control icons in an Accent color taken from it. The Tray app scales the Artwork once to the exact size the Device draws it (at most 352 pixels a side; smaller with Lanczos3 in linear light, larger with Mitchell, at most twice its size), so non-square Artwork such as 16:9 video thumbnails is shown whole and undistorted. The Accent is the most colorful hue of the Artwork at a medium saturation, made paler only as far as it has to (white for a gray Artwork). The Backdrop is the Artwork blurred and then darkened until white text has at least 4.5:1 contrast on every part of it and the Accent has 4.5:1 on a control's circle anywhere on it. A pressed control shrinks a little rather than lighting up, so the Backdrop needs no extra darkening for it: a bright cover's Backdrop stays at about sRGB 100 at its brightest with a white Accent and about 60 with a colored one. Artwork is identified by its content, and the Device shows an Artwork only with a Now Playing that names it: a new image under an unchanged title still replaces the old one, but while a player such as Tidal reports the new title with the old image, the old image shows under the new title until the new one arrives. The Device keeps the last 4 Artworks and asks the Tray app only for one it does not have; images travel lossless (QOI), and Artwork that does not fit the Device's screen is refused. When the Tray app cannot read an Artwork, the Device shows the song without Artwork rather than an empty box. On Windows, Now Playing reaches the Device without waiting for its Artwork: play/pause and a new title go out at once with the Artwork already known for the song (none for a new song), and again once the thumbnail is read. The thumbnail is read again only when the Media session changes or the player reports new media properties (so a second change under the same title still brings the new image), and a thumbnail that cannot be read in full within 1 second keeps the song's previous Artwork (protocol version 5).
- A moment of nothing playing between songs no longer shows on the Device: some players clear the title briefly during a song change, so the Tray app holds back nothing playing for 0.5 seconds and drops it when a new title arrives in that time. Nothing playing that lasts shows after those 0.5 seconds; a new title, play or pause always shows at once. This applies to every player and platform.
- Progress and seek on the Device: Now Playing shows a progress bar in the Accent with the position and the duration below it (`5:31`, or `1:05:03` from an hour on). The Device moves the position on by its own clock while playing, at the player's playback rate (a podcast at 1.5x moves 15 seconds in 10), four times a second, and stops it while paused, so the bar moves smoothly even when the player reports its position rarely (browsers often only on play, pause and seek); a play or pause toggled on the Device stops or starts the bar at once and keeps it so until the player follows, and if the player ignored it, the bar shows where the song really is again. Tapping the bar seeks there: the Device shows the new position at once and keeps it until the player has moved there, or for 3 seconds if it does not; a new song shows its own position at once. When the Media session cannot seek, a tap does nothing. Nothing playing, a song without a duration (a live stream, say) and a song of no length show no bar. On Windows, the position comes from the Media session's timeline, moved on by the time since the player last reported it at the playback rate Windows reports (normal when it reports none); a player that pauses or resumes without reporting a new position carries on from where the song had got to, so the bar neither jumps back on a pause nor ahead on a resume. A timeline that cannot be read leaves the song without a bar, but its title, artist and play/pause still show. A new position goes out only when it is more than 1 second from where the Device has got to by itself; a new playback rate goes out at once. A seek never goes before the start or past the end of the song's timeline, and of several taps on the bar while the player is still busy, the last one is carried out. A Device app that starts later gets the position as it is then. Now Playing carries the position, the duration (more than zero and at most 24 hours), the playback rate (up to 8x) and whether the Media session can seek, and a message with anything else fails to read; the Device sends seeks to a position (protocol version 6).
