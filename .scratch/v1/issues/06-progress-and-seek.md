# 06: Progress and seek

**What to build:** The Now Playing screen shows position and duration with a progress bar that moves smoothly between updates, and tapping the progress bar seeks. Position travels with a timestamp so the Device can advance it locally while playing and freeze it while paused.

**Blocked by:** 01

**Status:** in-progress

- [x] Now Playing carries duration, position and the time the position was taken
- [x] Seam 3 test: with a fake clock, the progress advances while playing and stays still while paused
- [x] Seam 1 test: a seek action from the Device arrives as a `MediaSource` seek to the right position
- [x] Seam 3 test: tapping the progress bar at a point produces a seek to the matching position
- [x] Nothing playing (no title) shows no progress bar at all
- [x] Media sessions that cannot seek ignore the tap without error

## Comments

**2026-11-24, implementation.**

- Clocks: no timestamp travels. The Tray core keeps each position with the moment it was taken on the computer's monotonic clock (`media::Progress { position, at, duration, can_seek }`) and moves it on to the moment it sends (`NowPlaying::to_device(now)`); the Device takes the position it receives as current on arrival and moves it on by its own monotonic clock. So the computer's and the Device's wall clocks never need to agree (the Device's is not even set before 08), and the USB network adds about a millisecond. The Windows adapter is the only place a wall clock is read: `LastUpdatedTime` (UTC) is turned into an age against the computer's own wall clock, and that into an `Instant` (a date in the future, or none, counts as now). The Device daemon keeps Now Playing with the moment it arrived and hands a Device app that starts later the position moved on to then (`protocol::NowPlaying::aged`). So "the time the position was taken" is explicit on the computer and, on the wire, is the moment of sending by contract.
- Protocol version 6: `NowPlaying.progress: Option<Progress { position, duration, can_seek }>` (`None` for no duration: live streams, players that leave the timeline empty) and `InputAction::Seek(Duration)`. The seek is absolute: the Device knows the duration and shows the position, a tap means "go here", and an absolute position lands in the same place however far the song moved on while the seek travelled, and also if it arrives twice; a relative one would add the Device's error to the player's. `Progress::position_after` (moves on while playing, stays while paused, never past the duration) is the one rule both sides use.
- Can seek: carried in Now Playing (Windows: `PlaybackInfo.Controls.IsPlaybackPositionEnabled`). The Device does not offer seeking when it is false (the bar's touch area is disabled, and the Device app's model refuses too), so nothing is sent and nothing is logged. A seek that Windows still refuses (the flag changed in between) is `Refused` and logged at warn like the other controls.
- Tray core: `TransportControl::Seek(Duration)` goes through the existing control worker (queue of 1, so a seek waits behind a stalled control, and presses beyond one are dropped). The session sends a Now Playing that differs from the last one sent only in its position when that position is more than `POSITION_TOLERANCE` (1 s) from where the Device has got to by itself (`Sent::agrees`), so players that report every few seconds while playing do not make it send; a seek on the computer, a position change while paused, or any other change still goes out. Nothing playing going out resets this.
- Windows adapter: `windows_media/timeline.rs` (pure, 10 unit tests) turns the timeline (`StartTime`, `EndTime`, `Position`, `LastUpdatedTime`, in ticks of 100 ns) into progress: duration `EndTime - StartTime` (none when not positive), position from `StartTime` clamped into the song, dated back by its age. The adapter now also wakes on `TimelinePropertiesChanged`; that only wakes the read and does not set the flag that makes `known_artwork` read the thumbnail again, so timeline events never re-read Artwork. Seek: `TryChangePlaybackPositionAsync(StartTime + position)` on the session current at that moment, with the existing 2 s `CONTROL_TIMEOUT` and cancel.
- Device app: `progress.rs` keeps two positions. The reported one moves on while the Tray app says playing; the shown one moves on while the Device shows playing (so an optimistic pause stops the bar at once and an optimistic play starts it), and whenever both say the same playback the shown one is the reported one, so a toggle the player ignored ends with the bar where the song really is. A tap on the bar seeks to that fraction of the duration (whole milliseconds), shows it at once, and keeps it until a Now Playing arrives whose position is more than 1 s from where the reported position would be anyway (the player acted, or a new song), or until `OPTIMISTIC_HOLD` (3 s) runs out: the same rule as for play/pause. A Slint timer redraws the bar every `PROGRESS_TICK` (250 ms), and only while the shown position moves; while paused or at the end no timer runs. In the Simulator (debug build, desktop renderer) the moving bar cost about 11 ms of CPU per second. A duration of zero counts as none. `Screen::with_clock` takes the clock, for tests. `fake-now-playing` takes `--duration` and `--position`.
- Layout: the bar (6 px track, 32 px touch target) with the position left and the duration right below it (20 px white text) sits between the artist and the controls in the text column; the Accent fills the played part. The controls' top padding went from 24 to 12 px.
- Tests: protocol (`tests/progress.rs`: moves on while playing, stays while paused, stops at the end; round trips with progress and seeks). Seam 1: a seek from the Device arrives as `TransportControl::Seek` to the same position; a position reported 30 s before the session starts reaches the Device moved on by 30 s while playing and unchanged while paused; a report within 1 s of where the Device has got to is not sent, one elsewhere is; a new position while paused is sent. Device daemon: a Device app that starts 300 ms late gets the position moved on by at least that while playing, and unchanged while paused. Seam 3 with a fake clock (`device-app/tests/progress.rs`, 16 tests): position and duration shown; the bar moves within a second (not only when the second changes) and on; still while paused; stops at the end; a new position replaces the one moved on to; an optimistic pause stops it, an optimistic play moves it from where it was paused; a pause the player ignored shows where the song got to; no bar for nothing playing, no duration or a duration of zero; hours; a tap at a quarter seeks to 82.75 s of 331 s and moves on from there; a session that cannot seek ignores the tap; the next Now Playing corrects a seek; a Now Playing from before the seek keeps it until the hold runs out. Mutation-checked: no tick timer, the shown playback ignored, a report always ending the seek, no return to the reported position, a paused position that moves on: each makes a test fail. Removing only the Rust-side can-seek check does not (the disabled touch area already blocks the tap).

Verified by hand (debug builds, Simulator with `SLINT_SCALE_FACTOR=1`): the real Tray app read TIDAL's timeline (paused at 2:18 of 5:53, can seek) and the Simulator showed the bar at that point in the Accent with `2:18` and `5:53`; `fake-now-playing --duration 331 --position 40` showed the bar moving, `0:43` and three seconds later `0:46`. Not yet verified by hand: the bar moving with a real playing player (TIDAL was paused and was not to be touched), a browser that reports its timeline rarely, a seek arriving in a real Windows player, a player that reports `IsPlaybackPositionEnabled` as false, and the look and CPU cost on the real Device (12).

Open questions for the product: players with a playback rate other than 1 (podcasts at 1.5x) drift until their next report, since `PlaybackRate` is not carried; a player that changes its playback state without reporting a new timeline (pausing without updating the position) makes the bar jump back to its last reported position on pause, or ahead by the paused time on resume, until it next reports. Each timeline event reads the media properties once more (not the thumbnail), which is cheap but could be narrowed to the timeline if a player turns out to report very often.

**2026-11-24, review fixes (protocol, Device daemon, Device app).**

- Protocol (still version 6: unreleased, and tickets 04 and 05 bumped once per ticket, not per review fix): `Progress` moved to `protocol/src/progress.rs` with private fields, getters and `Progress::new(position, duration, rate, can_seek) -> Result<_, ProgressError>`: the duration is more than zero and at most `MAX_DURATION` (24 hours), a position past the duration is taken as the end. A message is checked the same way when it is read (`#[serde(try_from = "UncheckedProgress")]`, like `Artwork`). New `PlaybackRate`: a `u16` of thousandths, 1 to 8000 (up to 8x), checked on reading too; `from_f64` rounds to thousandths and refuses non-finite, zero, negative and above 8x. `position_after` now moves on by elapsed times the rate in `u128` nanoseconds (no overflow, no panic for any elapsed time), never past the duration; `with_position` and `after` build on it. The "duration of zero counts as none" filter in the Device app is gone: the type rules it out. So the rate the playback-rate open question asked about is now carried, and the Device follows it.
- Device app: `progress.rs` keeps each anchor as a `protocol::Progress` plus the `Instant` it was there and moves it on only with `position_after`, so both sides use one rule (the anchor's own overflowing `+` is gone). A tap's position is computed in whole milliseconds from a finite, clamped fraction (`seek_position`); a fraction that is not a number (a bar of no width) seeks nowhere instead of panicking in `Duration::mul_f64`. A report that leaves the position where it would be anyway now keeps the shown position also while a toggled play/pause waits for the player (paused at 0:40, Play tapped, a still-paused report at 0:40 no longer snaps the moving bar back); a report away from it ends the wait, as for a seek. Seek and toggle carry over only within the same song (same title and artist): a new song shows its own position at once. The seek goes through `act` like the other actions (the callback only works out the position), so `act` has no dead arm. `Screen::ticking` says whether the progress timer runs.
- Device daemon test: the lower bound of the late Device app's position allowed no slack for the daemon reading the message late (its clock starts at the read, the test's at the send); it now asks for at least half the wait. Paused tokio time was not used: it would auto-advance into the test's timeout while real loopback IO is pending.
- Tests added: protocol (`tests/progress.rs`, 17): 1.5x moves 15 s in 10, 0.5x moves 5 s in 10, `Duration::MAX` elapsed at 8x on a 24 h song stops at the end, position past the duration clamps (made and read), zero and over-24 h durations refused (made and read), rates 0 and 8001 refused when read, 1 and 8000 accepted, rates in thousandths, non-finite/zero/negative/above-8x rates not made; the round trips carry a 1.5x rate. Seam 3 (`device-app/tests/progress.rs`, now 22; the zero-duration test went with the filter): the bar moves at 1.5x; a still-paused report after Play keeps the bar moving; a seek and a pause together; a new song of the same length near the expected position while a seek waits shows the new song's position; a NaN tap sends nothing; a tap at the end of a 24 h song seeks to 24 h; the progress timer runs while playing and stops while paused, at the end and with nothing playing. The rate, still-paused-after-Play, new-song and NaN tests failed before the fixes.

**2026-11-24, review fixes (Tray core, Windows adapter).**

- Tray core: `media::Progress` is now `{ taken: protocol::Progress, at: Instant }` (so it carries the rate and is checked like the wire type) with `at_time(now, playback)`; `to_device` and the skip rule use it, so the Tray's own extrapolation follows the rate, and a rate change is not "the same but for the position": it goes out at once. `Sent` is folded into `Told` (`Something(Sent)`, `SomethingUntil(Sent, Instant)`), so after nothing playing went out there is nothing to compare against and the same song comes back to the Device; before, one untested `sent = None` kept the two in step. `fake-now-playing` also takes `--rate` (for example `1.5`). The control queue (`mpsc` of 1) became `WaitingControl` (one slot, `Mutex` + `Notify`): a seek replaces a seek that waits (latest wins), anything else waits only in an empty slot and is dropped otherwise, as before.
- Windows adapter: the timeline (`GetTimelineProperties`, its fields, `Controls.IsPlaybackPositionEnabled`) is read as its own result; an error is logged at debug and leaves Now Playing without progress (and without seeking), but the title, artist and playback still go out. `PlaybackInfo.PlaybackRate` (an `IReference<f64>`, null for players that never set it) is read; absent or unusable (not above zero, above 8x, not a number) counts as normal. `timeline::progress` now takes the playback info and returns the reported position moved on by its age (at the rate, while playing, never past the end) with `at = now`; the old `now.checked_sub(age).unwrap_or(now)` took a position too old for the monotonic clock as fresh. `ProgressFollower` keeps the last raw timeline (start, end, position, last updated) and playback info: an unchanged read returns the previous progress unchanged, so `publish` deduplicates again (before, `at` was recomputed every read, with jitter, and by the whole interval when `LastUpdatedTime` is 0); the same timeline with other playback info (a pause, resume or rate change the player did not report) carries the previous progress on to now under the old playback and rate, then on with the new ones. This rule lives in the adapter, not the Tray core, because "no new report" is only visible in the raw timeline; the Tray core sees a derived `at`. A failed timeline read and a new Media session start it over. Seek targets are clamped to `[StartTime, EndTime]` of the same timeline read. The adapter logs each timeline read at trace (`tray_app::windows_media=trace`).
- Tests added: Seam 1 (`tray-core/tests/now_playing.rs`, now 17): the same song after nothing playing went out is sent again; the same song back within the hold where the Device has got to is not sent; a pause within tolerance goes out; at 1.5x a position reported 30 s earlier reaches the Device moved on by 45 s; a report at 2x that agrees with the Device is not sent; a rate change goes out at once. Seam 1 (`playback_controls.rs`): of three seeks during a stalled control only the last is carried out (failed before the fix). Unit (`windows_media/timeline.rs`, now 21): moved on by its age while playing, still while paused, at the rate, stopped at the end for a position older than the monotonic clock; no progress for a duration over 24 h; rate absent/0/negative/NaN/9 counts as normal; seek clamped to start and end; an unchanged read gives the same progress; pause and resume without a new report do not jump; a rate change without a report moves on at the new rate from then; a new report is taken as reported; a failed read starts over. The tautological `the_progress_of_the_session_goes_with_now_playing` (a field copy) is gone.
- Seen by hand: the real Tray app against a playing Media session (app id `22EB8429C9C8096C`) read a timeline of `end: 0` (the player leaves it empty), so no bar, correctly; the rate read as normal, and no further Now Playing was published in 20 s (which does not prove the dedupe: Windows may not have woken the read in that time). The moving bar with a real player that reports a timeline is still not seen by hand.
